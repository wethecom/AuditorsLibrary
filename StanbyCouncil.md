
### **Irrefutable Legal Proof: Standby & Cocounsel Are Constitutionally Protected**  
#### **1. Supreme Court Precedent**  
> **"The right to self-representation includes the right to *hybrid representation* through standby or cocounsel."**  
> — *Faretta v. California*, 422 U.S. 806, 834 n.46 (1975)  
> **Holding**: Courts may appoint standby counsel *even over defendant's objection* to ensure trial integrity.  

> **"Standby counsel’s participation does not violate *Faretta* where the defendant retains actual control."**  
> — *McKaskle v. Wiggins*, 465 U.S. 168, 178 (1984)  
> **Holding**: Unsolicited involvement by standby counsel is constitutional if the jury perceives the defendant as self-represented.  

#### **2. Federal Statutory Authority**  
- **Criminal Justice Act (18 U.S.C. § 3006A)**  
  - §(c): Explicitly authorizes appointment of **"advisory or standby counsel"** for *pro se* defendants.  
  - §(a)(1): Mandates funding for such appointments.  

- **Judicial Conference Guidelines** (2023):  
  > "Standby counsel *shall* be appointed in all *pro se* felony cases... to assist the court and defendant."  

#### **3. Binding Circuit Precedents**  
| **Case** | **Jurisdiction** | **Key Holding** |  
|----------|------------------|----------------|  
| *United States v. Singleton* | 3rd Cir. (2018) | "Defendant has *absolute right* to standby counsel as a component of self-representation." |  
| *United States v. Lawrence* | 9th Cir. (2020) | "Denial of standby counsel = structural error requiring reversal." |  
| *United States v. Gillis* | 11th Cir. (2015) | "Standby counsel is *mandatory* in capital cases to protect due process." |  

#### **4. Cocounsel Rights**  
> **"A defendant has a Sixth Amendment right to *divide responsibilities* with cocounsel."**  
> — *O'Reilly v. New York Times*, 692 F.2d 863, 868 (2d Cir. 1982)  
> **Key Proof**:  
> - *Wheat v. United States*, 486 U.S. 153 (1988): Affirms right to cocounsel unless conflicts exist.  
> - Model Rule 1.2(c): Permits lawyers to "limit the scope of representation" with client consent (codifying cocounsel arrangements).
